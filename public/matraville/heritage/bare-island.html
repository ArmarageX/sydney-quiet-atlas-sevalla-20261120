Bare Island Fort: closure & mainland context

The bridge and island are not an available walk on the checked notice. This page explains the closure first, then the historical context visible in the retained photograph.

Closure comes before the itinerary

The official Destination NSW notice checked on 22 November 2026 says Bare Island is closed for bridge works and the fort is not open for bookings during the closure. It states construction starts on 24 August 2026 and is scheduled to finish by late 2027. A scheduled finish is not a guaranteed reopening date. Recheck the official notice before making any future plan.[1]

Understand the photograph

The historical photograph shows the island, fort and connecting bridge at La Perouse. It is not a representation of current access. Do not use people visible on that bridge as evidence that visitors may cross it now. Any mainland view should be from a currently permitted public location, without entering a work site or crossing a barrier.[1]

A compact historical reading

Destination NSW describes the fort as built in the early 1880s to protect Sydney’s back door. It says the fort operated until 1908 and later became a war veterans’ home. Those changes give the place a military and social-history context; they do not establish a current guided-tour programme or a visitor invitation during closure.[1]

Make a different plan during works

If the point of your outing is an accessible historic interior or a fort tour, choose another confirmed-open destination. The nearby coast can be considered separately, but this guide does not promise an unbroken walk around the works or recommend entering the surrounding water. A closure is a reason to change the itinerary, not to look for an unofficial way onto the island.
