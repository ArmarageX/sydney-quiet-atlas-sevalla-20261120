Matraville · local commemoration

Matraville Soldiers’ Settlement memorial plaque

A close look at a real commemorative inscription, not a speculative walking tour through private homes. The retained photograph lets the plaque itself anchor a small piece of local history.

Read what the plaque actually says

The photographed plaque is headed “World War I Battlefields of France”. It names the Matraville Soldiers’ Settlement Community Celebration and says it paid tribute to Australian soldiers who fought in World War I. The image is filed by its photographer as Soldiers’ settlement, Matraville, Sydney; the visible inscription, not an inferred building history, is the evidence used here.[1]

Follow the commemoration’s own explanation

The inscription describes signs in the City of Paris design being presented to Randwick City Council for streets named after France’s World War I battlefields and towns. It carries the date 28 June 2008 and a reference to the 90th anniversary of the Armistice. These are statements recorded on this plaque, not a claim that every street sign survives unchanged today.[1]

Keep remembrance separate from private access

The plaque offers a reason to notice the connection between place names and commemoration. It does not grant access to nearby houses, establish a public museum, or certify a signed heritage trail. Keep any visit to permitted public space and avoid treating residential gardens or entrances as historical exhibits open to visitors.

What remains to check before a visit

The attempted state-register URL returned a missing page, so this guide does not publish an unverified street address, monument opening hours or a precise walking circuit. If making a dedicated trip, confirm the plaque’s current location and public approach with council first. The historical photograph supports reading the inscription; it cannot certify the plaque’s present condition or a step-free route.
