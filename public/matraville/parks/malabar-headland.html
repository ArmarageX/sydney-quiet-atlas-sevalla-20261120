Malabar Headland National Park

A coastal landscape between two beaches, with an important distinction between its western and eastern sections. Plan around access restrictions rather than assuming every marked path is available.

Two sections, different access

NSW National Parks places this park between Maroubra and Malabar beaches. Its overview says the western section is open year-round, while the eastern section closes when ANZAC Rifle Range is in use. The practical first decision is therefore which section you intend to visit, not just where to park. Check the linked safety messages and alerts before travelling.[1]

Choose a track, not a vague coastal route

The official overview links Western Escarpment walking track and Boora Point walking track. Read the individual track information before choosing between them: this park overview is not a substitute for distance, grade and route-specific access. Leave a viable return plan if the eastern section is unavailable.[1]

Notice the vegetation as well as the view

The source describes sandstone escarpments, cliffs, heritage structures and remnant eastern suburbs banksia scrub, including critically endangered vegetation. Staying on designated paths is a way to experience that landscape without treating the scrub or cliff edges as an informal shortcut. Wildlife sightings are possibilities, not a promised part of a visit.[1]

A photo is not an access bulletin

The historical image records the named headland, not the current state of its gates or tracks. Carry water and sun protection, respect closures and turn back when a route is unavailable. Do not infer a continuous open walk from one attractive view, or assume that beach access and national-park access share the same rules.
